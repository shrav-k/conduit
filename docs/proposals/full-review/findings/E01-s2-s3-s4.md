Not binding. Where the verifier corrected something, the correction governs. [Index of all parts](README.md).

**The analyst's summary.** The pre-registration discipline is real: the three READMEs state their limits honestly, name their gaps, were accepted before any run and have not been edited since except for a two-line link. But what they register is mostly a programme of things to do and record, not conditions that can fail: P1 in each is a precondition (vectors that the gate already requires to pass), most other conditions are duties to record or confirm a stated limit, and no scenario has a baseline or a threshold. S2, the acceptance scenario, cannot fail on its purpose claim: its baseline probe (A6) was folded into its own runs, its adapter row became a second from-scratch server, its plain row (the existing tool) is unchosen, and Linear's own server and login sit in a side probe. S3's fault matrix is half vacuous with one engine as the only enforcement point, its owed-stop condition passes with a provider that stops nothing because both faults end the run first, and it asks the owner for 72 hand actions that a cut made on the Mac removes. S4 is the best designed and the nearest to runnable, but its client axis is simulated: nothing in the repository shows a real client sending an idempotency key, so half its matrix describes a consumer that exists only as the scripted client, and its one real replay cannot fail anything. Real clients are spent where scripted ones suffice (second authors' runs, fault cells, whole replays) and withheld where only they can answer (do clients re-send by themselves; what can their own tools read). As registered the three scenarios cost about 11.5 hours of the owner's timed presence; the changes proposed bring that to about 5 and give each scenario a way to fail on its claim. I would run S4 first, restore S2's baseline, test S3's dangerous ordering (delivered, cut before the effect, recorded as not occurred, repeated) on the real pair, and take three results now that need no unwritten engine order. Thirteen lane orders built servers and a provider that the engine has not yet called once; the lane's next work should produce dated evidence, not more apparatus.
