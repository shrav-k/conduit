## FR-570

**IN_FORCE's narrowing sentence reverses the order in the ruling it cites: ruling 41 item 3a says open the narrower Conduit, then close the wider one**

GL7/F1 · defect · verifier: known, medium · decides: coordinator · known-new-situation

**Promises.** ACC-011, LIF-001, LIF-041, IFC-014, ACC-053

**Where.** IN_FORCE.md:20

**Quote.** "To narrow an access, close the Conduit and open a narrower one (15, as amended by 39 point 7 and 41 item 3a; CON-001, ACC-011)."

**Situation.** The line cites ruling 41 item 3a. The owner's accepted words there are (docs/proposals/principles-review/README.md:193-195): "narrowing becomes opening a narrower Conduit and closing the wider one, which an agent with the right to open can still do (preservation test 5)". The line, ACC-011 (spec/conventions/access.yaml:196-198, "the Conduit is closed and a narrower one ... is opened") and LIF-001 (spec/conventions/lifecycle.yaml:29-31) give the opposite order. Since tombstones were written, the order decides the result. Close first: the close leaves a tombstone, and LIF-041 (spec/conventions/lifecycle.yaml:862-864) gives conflict for any Conduit with "the same consumer and either the same local name or the same target and capability", so an Agent whose `access` Conduit lists open and close but not `lift.v1` can remove access and cannot narrow it. Open first, as the owner's words have it: the narrower Conduit is registered and opened under another local name (IFC-014), the wider one is then closed, and LIF-041 does not touch a Conduit that is already open (lifecycle.yaml:871). First-pass finding L02/F9 recorded the conflict and proposed make-before-break as a new wording. What it does not have: the cited ruling already states that order, so the line and the two observables differ from the owner's words, and the fix needs no ruling. The line and both promise observables are the ones to change.

**Counterargument.** Ruling 19's RR14 says "Narrowing one means revoking it and issuing another" (docs/proposals/relationships/README.md:83), so the owner's words exist in both orders, written before tombstones made the order matter. The line binds nothing, and LIF-041 decides the outcome either way. Opening first forces a second local name, so the consumer's tool names change.

**Least change.** IN_FORCE.md:20, ACC-011's observable, LIF-001's observable and spec/definitions.md:430: "To narrow an access, open a narrower Conduit and then close the wider one (41 item 3a). A Conduit made after the close needs the closed one's tombstone lifted (LIF-041, ACC-053)." No behavior is added.

**Precedent.** Make before break: Terraform's create_before_destroy; in Kubernetes RBAC and AWS IAM the narrower binding is created before the wider one is deleted.

**Who decides.** coordinator. AGENTS.md, What holds, point 2 (a line that differs from the owner's words is a defect); rulings 37 and 46 point 4 (the fix adds no behavior).

**Verifier.** known, medium. The quote is verbatim at IN_FORCE.md:20. First-pass finding L02/F9 already reports the substance: after a close, LIF-041 reports conflict until a lift. It proposes the same make-before-break wording with the same precedent, and its verifier's corrections say "Add to the fix IN_FORCE.md:20, which carries the same sentence". The one new claim, that ruling 41 item 3a sets the order open then close, does not hold up. Item 3a's words sit in the recommendation's "What it gives up" clause (docs/proposals/principles-review/README.md:193-195: "narrowing becomes opening a narrower Conduit and closing the wider one, which an agent with the right to open can still do"). They join two acts with "and" and do not set an order. The owner's words for ruling 41 are "Accept all" (docs/proposals/ruling-log.md:49). Ruling 19's RR14, also accepted, gives the other order: "Narrowing one means revoking it and issuing another" (docs/proposals/relationships/README.md:83). The ledger's own record of how 41 item 3a amended ruling 15 matches the line: "To narrow an access, an actor closes the Conduit and opens a narrower one (ACC-011)" (docs/proposals/ruling-log.md:469). So the line does not differ from the owner's words as the ledger records them. One more claim is wrong: lifecycle.yaml:870-871, "Opening an already open Conduit succeeds under ACC-006 and is not blocked", is about reopening the same Conduit, not a sibling. Opening first works only because the wider Conduit leaves no tombstone until it is closed.

**Verifier's corrections.** This is not a case of the line differing from the owner's words. It is L02/F9 (defect, medium, the coordinator decides the wording; whether narrowing should cost a lift at all is the owner's, with finding 4-01). F1's fix list is incomplete. The same close-then-open sentence is also at spec/definitions.md:192 ("Access is narrowed by closing a Conduit and opening one that lists fewer operations or applies further filters") and in the ledger's amendment table at docs/proposals/ruling-log.md:469. F1 names definitions.md:430 but neither of those. Neither L02/F9 nor F1 names :192 or :469.


## FR-571

**Ruling 21's DD5 (a path in an instance is absolute) has no line in IN_FORCE, and INS-018 is weaker: a relative path registers and means a different directory on each engine**

GL7/F2 · gap · verifier: weakened, low · decides: coordinator · new

**Promises.** INS-018, PLC-020, CTR-014

**Where.** spec/promises/instantiation.yaml:258

**Quote.** "The value reaches its destination exactly as supplied. Whether a relative path is then valid is decided by the schema of the type that receives it."

**Situation.** The owner accepted DD5 under ruling 21 (docs/proposals/declarations/README.md:57): "(a) Not allowed in an instance. A path argument is absolute, and a frontend may resolve a relative path before submitting." Ruling 21 is in force, amended only by 41 item 10, and the record's note on later rulings changes DD3 and DD4 only. IN_FORCE.md cites ruling 21's DD1, DD3, DR1 to DR4, DR7 to DR10 and DR12 (lines 63, 65, 66, 67, 157) and has no line for DD5. INS-018 forbids only the join. PLC-020 gives a meaning only to "An absolute path in the configuration of a Resource" (spec/promises/placement.yaml:290). DL16 leaves the process's "working directory" to each engine (spec/implementation-defined.yaml:174). So `config: {root: repo}` on a directory Resource whose Type says `{type: string}`, or `directory: .` for an Agent's own tools (CTR-014: "Every string counts", spec/promises/contracts.yaml:352), registers on every engine and names a different directory under each engine and each runtime provider. The documents do not determine what is shared, which is what DD5 was accepted to prevent ("the submitter's working directory means nothing"). The shipped packages refuse a relative path only at run time (packages/provider-file/src/lib.rs:32-34; docs/work-orders/076-agents-v2-folder-grant.md:31); nothing asks it of an outside package. The promise is the weaker text; IN_FORCE lacks the line.

**Counterargument.** The language cannot tell which string is a path, so only a Type's schema can hold DD5, and INS-018 is the recorded fix of defect L84 (docs/LANGUAGE_DEFECTS.md:219), accepted with the logged decisions under ruling 39 point 6. The first-party packages already require absolute paths. Not marked unsafe because the engine shares what the author wrote; it is the meaning of what was written that varies.

**Least change.** (1) IN_FORCE.md topic 4, one line: "A path in an instance is absolute and the engine joins no base (21 DD5; INS-018, PLC-020). Coordinator's: the Type's schema holds the rule." (2) In INS-018's or CTR-004's text, at level should: a Type whose configuration holds a path tied to a node states a schema that accepts only an absolute path, so a relative one is rejected at registration and not at setup. No engine behavior is added.

**Precedent.** Kubernetes validates hostPath and mountPath as absolute paths; systemd requires absolute paths in ExecStart= and WorkingDirectory=; Docker requires an absolute container path for a volume.

**Who decides.** coordinator. Rulings 37 and 46 point 4; ruling 71 point 4 for the should; logged for the owner to strike. If the owner wants engine-level rejection (a core absolute-path schema), that part is the owner's.

**Verifier.** weakened, low. The quote is verbatim at spec/promises/instantiation.yaml:258-259. DD5 is as quoted at docs/proposals/declarations/README.md:57, and ruling 21's ledger row reads "in force; amended by 41 (item 10)" (docs/proposals/ruling-log.md:29). The spec text the finding calls weaker was accepted by the owner: INS-018 has this wording in the first numbered spec, commit e271d1c7, the same commit that wrote DD5's record. The defects register maps L84 to it (docs/LANGUAGE_DEFECTS.md:219: "ruled: INS-018"), and ruling 39 point 6 says the specification's standing is that "the owner accepted it as it stands at ruling 39" (docs/proposals/ruling-39/README.md:40-41). The situation is not undecided. The engine passes the value as written (INS-018; PLC-020 at spec/promises/placement.yaml:288-291). Validity is for the Type's schema (INS-018). For the own-tools directory, CTR-014 assigns it outright: "Every string counts, the empty string among them: what an implementation does with a directory it cannot use belongs to the Type's contract" (spec/promises/contracts.yaml:351-353). Two engine builders do not diverge. IN_FORCE.md:63 covers INS-018 inside "INS-001 to INS-021" but cites "21 DR1 to DR4" and not DD5; `grep -n DD5 IN_FORCE.md` returns nothing. DD5 is not the only uncited item: DD2, DR5, DR6, DR11 and DR13 also have no citation. No known-open record has the point.

**Verifier's corrections.** This is a pointer omission, not a gap in spec/. The text says who judges a relative path (the Type's schema and contract), and the owner accepted that text at ruling 39 point 6. What remains: (1) IN_FORCE.md:63 could cite "21 DD5" beside INS-018. (2) The proposed should-level rule for Types is an optional improvement, not a fix. The first-party Types already require absolute paths (packages/provider-file/src/lib.rs:33; docs/work-orders/076-agents-v2-folder-grant.md:31). The one place a reader can be misled is inspection showing a relative own-tools reach under CTR-014. The coordinator decides; nothing here is the owner's.
