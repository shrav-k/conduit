# 010 — Second reading, 2026-11-16: two subscriptions with each vendor

A Codex worker read the pages on 2026-11-16 under work order 190. Quotations are from the
pages as served that day. This note decides nothing; accounts are named by label only, primary
and second. This is a reading of public documents, not legal advice.

## The case

One person, the owner, holds two subscriptions with each vendor: a primary and a second
Claude subscription with Anthropic, and a primary and a second ChatGPT subscription with
OpenAI. Each Agent is an unmodified Claude Code or Codex in a home of its own, signed in
through the vendor's own flow to the one account its configuration names; Conduit never
holds or passes the login ([ruling 49](../../docs/proposals/ruling-49/README.md);
[ruling 75](../../docs/proposals/ruling-75/README.md) C3). Several such Agents run at once and for
days: A5's stage 3 is a 48-hour soak of small scripted turns, and Conduit's own work orders
run through Agents of the same kind ([ruling 70](../../docs/proposals/ruling-70/README.md)). The
second accounts are used only through Conduit, as a test
([ruling 72's later answers](../../docs/proposals/ruling-72/README.md#later-answers)).

## What the sources say

### 1. More than one subscription for one person

**Anthropic.** “To access Claude Pro and other subscription services we may make available to
individuals, you must sign up for a subscription with us ... first by creating an Account, and
then following the subscription procedure on our Services.” (AN2)

“You may not share your Account login information, Anthropic API key, or Account credentials
with anyone else. You also may not make your Account available to anyone else.” (AN2)

“To stay signed in to multiple accounts at once, such as work and personal accounts, give each
account its own configuration directory.” (AN4)

As a summary, AN4 documents multiple account sign-ins; AN2 addresses subscriptions for
individuals and bars making an account available to someone else. The pages read say nothing
on this: an express allowance, number limit or prohibition for one person purchasing two paid
subscriptions of the same kind. They do not call that purchasing case ordinary usage.

**OpenAI.** The pages read say nothing on this. OA1 and OA2 could not be read (HTTP 403).
OA3 and OA4 describe account-based automation, without a statement about the number of
subscriptions one person may hold or an account being for one person.

### 2. A second subscription and the usage limits

**Anthropic.** The closest sentence specifically about subscription allowances is:
“Advertised usage limits for Pro, Max, and Ultra plans assume ordinary, individual usage of
Claude Code and the Agent SDK.” (AN1)

In AN2, the closest complete sentence on bypassing protections is: “You also must not abuse,
harm, interfere with, or disrupt our Services, including, for example, introducing viruses or
malware, spamming or DDoSing Services, or bypassing any of our systems or protective measures.”
(AN2)

Under the heading Do Not Abuse Our Platform, AN3 lists: “Coordinate malicious activity across
multiple accounts, or create multiple accounts, to avoid detection, circumvent product
guardrails, or generate identical or similar inputs that otherwise violate our Usage Policy”
(AN3). It also lists: “Intentionally bypass or interfere with capabilities, restrictions, or
guardrails established within our products, including by circumvention of the account sign-up
or log-in process or by instructing the model to produce harmful outputs (e.g., jailbreaking or
prompt injection), without prior authorization from Anthropic” (AN3).

These are the sources' allowance assumption and protection/abuse provisions. None of the
passages read expressly says whether a second paid subscription may extend one person's
allowance. This note does not turn their silence into permission or apply their general words
to the case itself.

**OpenAI.** The pages read say nothing on this. OA1 and OA2 could not be read (HTTP 403), so
there is no checked terms or policy sentence on circumventing usage or rate limits here.
OA3 describes runners for “users who need ChatGPT/Codex rate limits instead of API key usage.”
(OA3) That compares authentication routes; it does not discuss combining subscriptions.

### 3. Several Agents, at once and for days

**Anthropic.** AN2's prohibited-use list includes: “Except when you are accessing our Services
via an Anthropic API Key or where we otherwise explicitly permit it, to access the Services
through automated or non-human means, whether through a bot, script, or otherwise.” (AN2)

AN1 says OAuth “is designed to support ordinary use of Claude Code and other native Anthropic
applications.” (AN1) Its context includes the ordinary, individual usage sentence in question 2,
and: “Developers building products or services that interact with Claude's capabilities,
including those using the Agent SDK, should use API key authentication through Claude Console
or a supported cloud provider.” (AN1)

AN1 also says: “Nor does it prevent an end user from signing in to the unmodified Claude Code
binary with their own Claude subscription, including where a platform hosts Claude Code as
described under Can customers offer Claude Code in their products? above.” (AN1)

As a summary, AN1 addresses native sign-in and developers, while AN2 states an automation
restriction with an explicit-permission exception. Neither passage states that several
scripted subscription sessions running continuously for 48 hours, or over days, fall within that
exception or the usage assumption. No concurrency count or continuous-duration allowance was
found in the parts read.

**OpenAI.** “Non-interactive mode lets you run Codex from scripts (for example, continuous
integration (CI) jobs) without opening the interactive TUI.” (OA3)
“codex exec reuses saved CLI authentication by default.” (OA3)

“API keys are the right default for automation because they are simpler to provision and
rotate. Use this path only if you specifically need to run as your Codex account.” (OA3)
“This is an advanced workflow for enterprise and other trusted private automation. API keys
are still the recommended option for most CI/CD jobs.” (OA4)
“Do not use this workflow for public or open-source repositories.” (OA3)

As a summary, these pages document scripted runs and a qualified ChatGPT-managed authentication
workflow. OA4's concurrency/file rules appear in question 5. Neither states an allowance for
several independently signed-in Agents working without a break over days. OA1 and OA2 could
not be read (HTTP 403); the terms on automated or programmatic use are still unchecked.

### 4. Who may use an account

**Anthropic.** AN2's account-sharing sentences are quoted in question 1. It also says:
“You are responsible for all activity under the account through which you access the Services.”
(AN2)

“Each end user must authenticate with their own Anthropic API key, Claude subscription plan
credentials, or 3P inference provider credential (Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud's Agent Platform,
Microsoft Foundry).” (AN1)

“Anthropic does not permit third-party developers to offer Claude.ai login into their own
applications, or to route requests through Free, Pro, Max, or Ultra plan credentials on behalf
of their users.” (AN1)
“Moreover, developers may not collect, store, or intermediate Claude.ai credentials or session
tokens — sign-in to a Claude account must complete through Anthropic's own flow.” (AN1)

AN3 says: “Users are responsible for ensuring that agents they build or deploy, including
actions those agents take through tools, browsers, or connected systems, comply with this
Usage Policy.” (AN3)

As a summary, the pages distinguish another person's access, third-party credential handling,
native end-user sign-in and responsibility for agents. The holder's own automation is subject
to AN2's exception wording quoted in question 3; this reading does not settle that exception.

**OpenAI.** “... like a password: it contains access tokens. Don’t commit it, paste it into
tickets, or share it in chat.” (OA3) OA4 has the same words. As a summary, this is a
credential-file handling rule, not the OA1 account-sharing term, which remains unchecked.

“Do not share the same file across concurrent jobs or multiple machines.” (OA4)
“Do not store auth.json in the repository, logs, or public artifact storage.” (OA4)
These are auth-file handling rules, not a checked terms statement about another person using
an account. OA3 and OA4 document the holder's account-based automation with question 3's
qualifications. OA1 and OA2 could not be read (HTTP 403); the earlier account-credential-sharing
claim remains unchecked against OA1.

### 5. One account in several homes or machines

**Anthropic.** “Each directory has its own settings, session history, and claude.ai login or
API key.” (AN4) This describes separate directories for different accounts, in question 1's
context. The pages read say nothing on this: one account signed in independently on several
clients, homes or machines at once. AN1's native sign-in sentence does not specify that case.

**OpenAI.** “Use one auth.json per runner or per serialized workflow stream.” (OA4)
“Do not share the same file across concurrent jobs or multiple machines.” (OA4)
A condition for OA4's workflow is: “only one machine or serialized job stream will use a given
auth.json copy” (OA4). Its reasons for reseeding include: “another machine or concurrent job
rotated the token first” (OA4).

As a summary, these sentences concern a given file/copy. They do not expressly address the
case of each home or machine completing a separate sign-in to the same account. The pages read
say nothing on this separate-sign-in concurrency question; OA1 and OA2 could not be read
(HTTP 403).
