## Counts

Standings count each promise once. “Holds” means only the bounded projection in its source
accounting, with the order's applied reading, not every clause of the promise. A contested
standing records a finding involving that promise even where its individual predicate holds;
the note distinguishes a refuted predicate, a conflicting reading and supporting context.
This avoids counting a local success as resolution of an outstanding composition finding.
The named model is the later order's accounting entry; earlier models remain in the notes.

## Outside the 217

Ruling 39's seven convention files also contain three **language-layer** safety promises:
**LIF-002, LIF-007 and IMP-003**. The catalogue therefore counts 128 safety promises in those
files, of which 125 belong to the convention layer. G5's account as ruled by ruling 75 E1
does not include these three. They are neither omitted convention promises nor extra rows.

## The account

Module and property labels follow the README's own keys; `module::key` below is a reference
to that documented property, not a claim about a Rust symbol. The modules are
[operations](src/operations.rs), [revisions](src/revisions.rs), [access](src/access.rs),
[placement](src/placement.rs), [gates](src/gates.rs), [secrets](src/secrets.rs),
[lifecycle](src/lifecycle.rs), [controls](src/controls.rs) and [records](src/records.rs).

without dispatch, C changed contents conflict, K keyless reachability, V revision checked

README, since it has no accounting list. MC7a's REV-010 supersedes MC2's excluded slice;
MC6 supersedes MC3's eight lifecycle deferrals. Supporting mentions alone do not replace a
later explicit accounting row.

in the source README still apply: these are finite reference models, not engine checks,
